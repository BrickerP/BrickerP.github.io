# GitHub Profile refinement — Problem definition

Status: approved on 2026-07-31

Decision date: 2026-07-31

## Decision carried forward

The primary audience is AI builders and creative technologists who may follow,
discuss, or collaborate. Recruiters are a secondary audience. The Profile's job
is recognition and routing, not exhaustive proof.

The chosen direction remains a creator operating surface:

**one signature visual + two inspectable works + one living signal + one
collaboration invitation.**

The creator-facing identity system is now explicitly:

> **LOOP / LEDGER**
>
> **Strange loops. Open ledgers.**

## Current narrative conflict

The current Profile already contains two strong works and a strong line:
“I build systems you can enter — and systems you can audit.” The conflict is
the order and weight of the surrounding claims:

Source snapshot:
[BrickerP/BrickerP README](https://github.com/BrickerP/BrickerP/blob/main/README.md).

- job title, location, work authorization, resume, and LinkedIn frame the visit
  as recruiting before the creative premise has landed;
- the two flagship cards express a memorable Enter / Audit contrast, but the
  repeated link row immediately restates their actions;
- three equally detailed “Selected systems” begin a second portfolio instead of
  supporting the main signature;
- there is no direct, human invitation for the primary audience to start a
  conversation;
- AI Usage is intentionally a usage trace, not a productivity claim, so its
  cache disclosure and updated timestamp must remain part of its meaning.

The result is credible but split: visitors can see capability, yet may not
retain a single reason to follow or collaborate.

The first proposed correction—“Visual systems you can enter. Public traces of
how they were made.”—was accurate but still sounded like professional
positioning. It described the work without creating a persona someone would
repeat. The revised problem therefore includes **memorability and cultural
signal**, not only clarity.

## Look inward — self-questioning

### Are we already trapped by the two-card solution?

Partly. The durable idea is the **choice between entering a work and inspecting
its trace**, not a specific two-column layout. Step 4 may stack, pair, or
sequence the works as long as their conceptual equality remains.

### Are we assuming more proof creates more trust?

No. More equal-weight projects increase scanning cost. Trust should come from
two deep, live artifacts; secondary work only needs to show that the practice
extends beyond them.

### Are token totals evidence of productivity?

No. They show cadence, tool mix, and the scale of recorded traffic. The Profile
must never imply that more tokens equal more output, quality, or impact.

### What does “audit” promise here?

It means a visitor can inspect the measurement boundary, cache disclosure,
freshness, source code, and known limitations. It does not promise a security
audit, a complete work log, or proof of productivity.

### Does prioritizing creators mean hiding employability?

No. Role, location, work authorization, resume, and LinkedIn remain useful
background. They become available evidence after the creator premise rather
than the premise itself.

### Does the Profile need a manually updated “Now” feed?

No. A stale current-state claim is worse than no feed. AI Usage is already the
truthful living signal; additional recency content is deferred until it has a
durable source and update rule.

### Are “strange loops” and “open ledgers” too performative?

They would be if invented before the work. Here, LOOP 01 and LEDGER 02 already
exist. The phrase names an observable pattern instead of assigning a grand title
such as “AI visionary” or “thought leader.”

### Why not say “keep the receipts”?

It is punchier, but it risks presenting token traffic as proof of output or
winning an argument. “Open ledger” keeps the internet-native rhythm while
preserving the Chronicle's actual evidence boundary.

### Is this “AI influencer” enough?

Yes in the durable sense: it provides a repeatable phrase, a recurring motif, a
living signal, and a recognizable invitation. It deliberately excludes the
fragile influencer layer—follower counters, urgency, newsletter funnels, and
borrowed social proof.

## Look outward

### Who experiences the problem?

An AI builder encounters BrickerP from a repository, shared link, contribution,
or search result. They are deciding whether this person has a distinctive point
of view, makes real things, and is someone worth following or contacting.

### When does it hurt?

It hurts during the first short scan. Employment signals, two visual artifacts,
three project descriptions, and several destinations all compete before the
visitor has formed a stable mental model.

### Who benefits from the current state?

Recruiters benefit from immediate work-authorization and resume visibility.
That remains a valid secondary path, but its current priority optimizes the page
for the wrong first decision.

## Jobs to be done

### Functional job

“When I discover this person through their work, help me quickly understand
what they uniquely make, inspect credible examples, and find a low-friction way
to contact them.”

### Social job

“Help me feel confident that sharing or introducing this person will lead
others to distinctive, substantive work rather than a generic portfolio.”

### Emotional job

“Give me curiosity first and confidence second, without making me feel that I
am reading a pitch, resume, or vanity-metrics dashboard.”

### Top pains

1. Too many equally weighted claims before the visitor understands the theme.
2. Strong artifacts without an explicit collaboration invitation.
3. Career proof arriving before creative recognition.
4. Risk of interpreting a large token number as a claim about productivity.
5. Manual “current” content creating future staleness.

### Expected gains

1. One memorable sentence that connects the visual work and public trace.
2. Two obvious, credible paths for deeper exploration.
3. One human path to a collaboration conversation.
4. Hiring evidence that remains easy to find without steering the first read.
5. A Profile that stays truthful when nobody manually updates it for months.

## Problem statement

AI builders evaluating BrickerP as someone to follow or collaborate with cannot
quickly identify the distinctive practice connecting the work or how to start a
conversation, because career credentials and multiple forms of project proof
compete as equal claims. They leave with fragments of capability instead of a
memorable reason to return.

## How might we

How might we help an AI builder recognize a distinctive creative practice,
inspect two credible examples, and initiate a conversation during one brief
visit, while keeping hiring evidence available but secondary?

## Narrative hierarchy contract

This is a content order, not a layout decision:

1. **Identity thesis:** the distinctive practice in one memorable thought.
2. **Collaboration invitation:** what kinds of conversations are welcome.
3. **Two flagship proofs:** Endless Second Ring and AI Usage, equal in
   importance and different in purpose.
4. **Breadth proof:** no more than three stable links showing the practice
   extends beyond the flagships.
5. **Professional background:** role, location, work authorization, resume, and
   LinkedIn as compact secondary evidence.

Recommended thesis:

> **Strange loops. Open ledgers.**

Recommended explanatory line:

> I build strange AI loops and keep the ledger open.

Recommended invitation:

> Building a strange AI experiment—an agent tool or visual world? Open a line.

## Copy budget

The Profile should feel authored but sparse. Excluding text already rendered
inside the SVG cards and accessibility text:

| Content | Budget | Rule |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Identity thesis | 3–10 words | Repeatable personal-IP hook, no job-title stack |
| Explanatory line | 8–14 words | Connect the hook to the actual practice |
| Collaboration invitation | 10–16 words | Name no more than two territories |
| Flagship context | 10–14 words each | Explain meaning, do not repeat card copy |
| Breadth proof | Up to 3 × 8 words | Stable nouns and outcomes, no mini case studies |
| Professional background | Up to 18 words | One compact secondary line |
| **Total visible authored prose** | **85–105 words** | Hard ceiling: 120 words |

Additional rules:

- do not repeat token totals, cache percentages, dates, or tool names outside
  the generated AI Usage card;
- do not repeat an action immediately below a card that already contains it;
- use at most one sentence per narrative job;
- avoid “passionate,” “innovative,” “10x,” “thought leader,” and similar
  unsupported identity language;
- prefer concrete nouns—visual systems, agent systems, research tools, public
  traces—over a long technology inventory.

## CTA contract

There is one primary relationship action:

> **OPEN A LINE ↗**

- Destination: `mailto:yplmicro@gmail.com`.
- The invitation sentence explains why someone might write.
- Resume and LinkedIn are secondary professional actions.
- Each flagship keeps its own artifact action, but those are exploration
  routes, not competing relationship CTAs.
- No newsletter, booking funnel, social follow wall, or generic “Contact me.”

## Distinctiveness contract

Step 4 must make the identity visible without adding a new content system:

1. The two works are named **LOOP 01** and **LEDGER 02**.
2. One line-to-skyline gesture visually connects them: road trajectory becomes
   data baseline, with one vermilion handoff point.
3. AI Usage remains the only living signal; its updated time proves report
   freshness, not productive activity.
4. The tone may be playful and slightly cryptic, but every phrase must resolve
   into a real artifact within one action.
5. The Profile never calls itself an influencer, creator economy brand, lab,
   studio, or movement.

## Maintenance contract

### Automatic

- AI Usage light/dark SVGs regenerate with each successful AI Usage publish.
- The Profile continues referencing stable card URLs and requires no commit for
  routine data updates.
- Token totals, cache share, date range, and freshness stay owned by the
  generated card.

### Event-driven

- The Loop card changes only when the public identity or underlying artwork
  materially changes.
- Thesis and collaboration scope change only when the actual practice changes.
- Role, location, work authorization, resume, and LinkedIn change when their
  source facts change.
- A breadth-proof link is replaced only when another public work better
  represents the practice; the list never exceeds three.

### Lightweight review

Once per quarter, perform a five-minute truth check:

1. both flagship links and the collaboration email open;
2. resume and LinkedIn still resolve;
3. no prose contains a stale date, metric, “currently,” or unsupported claim;
4. no new widget or section has diluted the five-part hierarchy.

### Explicitly deferred

- “Recent signals” or “Now” content until it has an automatic, durable source;
- dynamic GitHub stats, trophies, streaks, language charts, or another card
  service;
- mirroring the personal site, resume, or AI Usage detail page inside README.

## Observable success signals

These can be self-tested before implementation is approved:

1. **Five-second recall:** after hiding the Profile, the tester can state both
   “strange loops” and “open ledgers.”
2. **Two-proof mapping:** the tester can explain that Loop proves an enterable
   visual system while AI Usage proves an inspectable process trace, and why
   they belong to one practice.
3. **Fifteen-second routing:** the tester can choose Loop or AI Usage without
   reading the breadth or background proof.
4. **Conversation path:** the tester can name one of the two welcome
   collaboration territories and find the action without opening LinkedIn or
   the resume.
5. **Evidence boundary:** the tester understands that AI Usage reports traffic
   with cache disclosure and does not claim productivity.
6. **Recruiter guardrail:** the tester can still reach resume and work-status
   information with one intentional follow-up.
7. **Maintenance dry run:** an AI Usage publish changes the Profile card without
   a Profile README commit.
8. **Truth audit:** leaving the authored Profile untouched for 90 days creates
   no false “latest,” hard-coded metric, or stale current-state claim.

## Step 3 recommendation

Approve the problem definition and content contract as the source for a single
Step 4 journey:

**recognize LOOP / LEDGER → choose a flagship → enter the world or inspect the
trace → open a line → optionally verify breadth and background.**
