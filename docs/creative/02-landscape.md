# GitHub Profile refinement — Landscape

Status: approved on 2026-07-31

Research date: 2026-07-31

## Audience decision

The Profile is primarily for AI builders and creative technologists who may
follow, discuss, or collaborate. Recruiter comprehension is useful but
secondary. The page should therefore behave like a compact creator surface,
not a resume, dashboard, or media funnel.

## Comparison set

### Simon Willison — activity is the proof

Source: [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/)

- The homepage starts with a searchable, dated stream rather than a long pitch.
- Stable routes such as Tools and TIL connect commentary to things that can be
  used or inspected.
- The useful pattern is a small, credible activity signal with direct links.
- Do not copy the volume or density of the feed; BrickerP's strongest evidence
  is visual work and an automatically published usage trace.

### Shawn “swyx” Wang — identity, current experiment, then archive

Source: [swyx.io](https://swyx.io/)

- A short identity statement explains the current body of work.
- One “Recent experiment” appears before the larger content archive.
- Projects, writing, speaking, and subscription paths are clearly separated.
- Borrow the current-experiment pattern for AI Usage, but avoid the newsletter
  funnel, social proof, and number of sections.

### Hamel Husain — a precise current thesis establishes trust

Source: [hamel.dev](https://hamel.dev/)

- The page states a specific present focus before listing dated work.
- A few concrete credentials support the thesis without replacing it.
- Borrow the order: what I am exploring now, how I work, then evidence.
- Do not lead with years of experience, employers, logos, or a skills matrix;
  those would turn a collaboration surface back into a recruiting page.

### Andrej Karpathy — human hook and explained artifacts

Source: [karpathy.ai](https://karpathy.ai/)

- The opening line is short, personal, and memorable.
- Teaching, writing, and projects are separated, and artifacts link to a live
  experience or source.
- Each BrickerP flagship should answer what it is, why it is interesting, and
  where to enter it.
- Karpathy's own “outdated” project notes are a useful warning: do not build a
  manually maintained full-history gallery.

### Jay Alammar — one visual promise becomes the brand

Source: [jalammar.github.io](https://jalammar.github.io/)

- “Visualizing machine learning one concept at a time” is a compact promise,
  and the artifacts repeatedly prove it in the same visual language.
- Borrow the discipline of naming what the visual is for; do not leave either
  SVG as unexplained decoration.
- The frozen-blog notice also shows why BrickerP should have one truthful,
  sustainable current signal rather than several “latest” modules.

## Pattern synthesis

The strongest pages do not try to prove everything above the fold. They use:

1. one specific point of view;
2. one current signal;
3. a small number of explained artifacts;
4. one obvious way to follow or collaborate;
5. a deeper archive outside the primary narrative.

The weak pattern for BrickerP would be an “AI portfolio” with an animated hero,
badges, GitHub metrics, a tool grid, a long biography, and several competing
calls to action. It would add information while making the person less
memorable.

## Trend correction — AI creator / influencer pages

The first comparison set skewed toward respected technical authors. That was
useful for clarity and credibility, but insufficient for a memorable internet
persona. A second pass looked at creator-led AI brands:

| Creator | What makes the persona repeatable | What to borrow | What not to borrow |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Ben Tossell](https://www.bentossell.com/) | His homepage is a command-line world, not a biography | One interface metaphor that immediately feels like the person | Terminal cosplay without a reason |
| [Pieter Levels](https://pieter.com/~pieter/) | A long-running, eccentric internet world full of real experiments | Visible taste, recurring motifs, and honest accumulated work | The volume, chaos, or deliberately hard-to-contact stance |
| [Matt Wolfe](https://mattwolfe.com/) | “AI news and tools filter” is easy to repeat and always current | One clear category promise and a visible cadence | Newsletter funnels, urgency headlines, and channel grids |
| [Greg Isenberg](https://www.gregisenberg.com/) | One phrase—startup ideas to win on the internet—organizes everything | A compact verbal hook plus one stable visual mark | Revenue bait, idea-count counters, and guide walls |
| [Allie K. Miller](https://www.alliekmiller.com/) | A named AI persona, strong social proof, and a direct work-with-me path | An explicit collaboration route | Logo walls, follower counts, courses, and consulting copy |

### 2025–2026 creator pattern

The transferable trend is not a specific visual style. It is a small identity
system:

1. a phrase someone else can repeat;
2. a recurring world or motif rather than a generic portfolio;
3. one living, dated artifact proving the practice continues;
4. one unmistakable relationship or conversion action;
5. social proof embedded in real output, not a trophy wall.

For BrickerP, the strongest proof is the work itself. Inventing follower
counters, a newsletter, a content feed, or an “AI thought leader” title would
look derivative rather than influential.

## Revised differentiation — LOOP / LEDGER

> **Strange loops. Open ledgers.**

Long-form identity line:

> **I build strange AI loops and keep the ledger open.**

This gives the two existing works a shared, repeatable name:

- **LOOP 01** — the 48-second Beijing night drive: enter the world.
- **LEDGER 02** — the AI Usage Chronicle: inspect the trace and its limits.

The phrase “open ledger” means disclosed measurement boundaries, cache share,
freshness, source, and limitations. It does not present token volume as an
achievement receipt or a productivity score.

The identity is specific enough for someone to say: “BrickerP makes strange AI
loops and leaves the ledger open.” That is more ownable than “AI engineer,”
“creative technologist,” or “building in public.”

### Signature visual premise for Step 4

A single warm-white road line runs toward the Loop card's vanishing point, then
turns into the Ledger card's skyline baseline. One restrained vermilion point
marks the handoff. The motif must work as two static SVGs; no neon glow,
gradient blob, robot imagery, or new dynamic service is required.

The Profile should read as a compact sequence:

1. **Personal IP** — LOOP / LEDGER plus the short repeatable hook.
2. **Signature work** — Endless Second Ring as the strange loop.
3. **Living proof** — AI Usage as the open ledger, with cache disclosure visible.
4. **Recent signals** — at most three meaningful builds or releases, each
   linking to an actual artifact; omit this section until it has a sustainable
   source.
5. **Contact and background** — GitHub and one collaboration route first;
   compact background information second.

## Do / do not

Do:

- keep the Enter / Audit diptych;
- name the pair LOOP 01 / LEDGER 02;
- use “Strange loops. Open ledgers.” as the repeatable creator hook;
- give each flagship one explanatory sentence and one primary action;
- use the Loop card and Token Skyline as the dominant visual vocabulary;
- let AI Usage remain the only automatically changing Profile image;
- make the collaboration invitation explicit and human.

Do not:

- add another live widget or dynamic card service;
- repeat token totals in surrounding prose;
- add badges, streaks, trophy walls, language charts, or a tool inventory;
- create a manually curated “latest” feed without an update rule;
- duplicate the personal site, Profile README, and AI Usage page.

## Step 2 recommendation

Proceed with a **personal world**, not a professional portfolio or media funnel:

**LOOP / LEDGER — one strange world, one open trace, one line of contact.**

The next creative step should define the exact narrative hierarchy, copy
budget, CTA, and maintenance rules before any Profile README implementation.
